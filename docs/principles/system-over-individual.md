# The system outlives the individual

**The rule:** knowledge held only in someone's head is a liability, not an asset. The system, not the individual, is the unit of capability.

## Why

An organisation that depends on a particular person to know what the customer evidence says has not built a capability — it has built a dependency. The knowledge leaves when they do, and in the meantime it cannot be checked, queried, delegated or handed to an agent.

The point is not that individual expertise does not matter. It is that expertise which is never externalised cannot compound, cannot be reviewed, and cannot be operated by anyone else. Making it portable is what turns it from personal skill into organisational capability.

## What it rules out

- Positions that exist only as someone's verbal explanation
- Processes documented as "ask X"
- Tooling and knowledge locked to one person's account, machine or workflow
- Approval chains that require a specific individual to interpret the evidence

## What it demands

Externalise the reasoning, not just the conclusion. A recorded position that does not say what it rests on cannot be revisited when the evidence changes — it can only be trusted or discarded. Documenting *why* is what makes a position maintainable by someone other than its author.

Portability is the practical test of this: knowledge in a plain, structured, version-controlled format travels between people, organisations and tools. Knowledge in a proprietary tool or a private folder does not.

## Test

Ask of any capability: *if the person most associated with this were unavailable for a month, what would stop?* Whatever stops is not yet in the system.
