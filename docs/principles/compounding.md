# Knowledge must compound

**The rule:** if a piece of work makes the next piece no cheaper, faster or better, the system has not done its job — regardless of how good that piece was on its own.

## Why

The problem Editorial Intelligence exists to solve is not a shortage of insight but the failure of insight to accumulate. Research is commissioned, evidence gathered, observations made — and then the learning disappears into a folder or an archived deck. Each new project starts roughly from scratch.

Volume does not fix this. Producing twice as much unaccumulated work doubles the cost and leaves the organisation in the same position. Only a change in the unit of work fixes it: the output of a cycle has to include something reusable, not just something publishable.

## What it rules out

- One-off deliverables with no reusable residue — a deck that answers a question and leaves no record of the reasoning
- Research that is not written back into the evidence base
- Formats that cannot be referenced later by a person or an agent
- "Refresh" work that regenerates a position rather than building on the recorded one

## What it demands

Every cycle should leave behind at least one of: a captured signal, a synthesised position, a documented framework, or evidence about what worked. If a piece of work leaves none of these, the compounding step was skipped and should be added before the work is considered finished.

## Test

Ask of any finished piece: *what does the next person — or the next agent — inherit from this?* If the honest answer is "the file", it did not compound.
